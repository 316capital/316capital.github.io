Common questions

What loans does 316 Capital arrange?

Business-purpose financing for real estate investors: fix and flip, bridge, ground-up construction, DSCR rental and BRRRR loans. We arrange financing through institutional capital partners and match each deal to the program that fits it.

Can I use a 316 loan on a home I will live in?

No. Our financing is for investment properties only, not owner-occupied primary residences. If you plan to live in the property, talk to a residential mortgage lender.

Where do you lend?

Nationwide. We arrange investor financing across the country, with deep roots in Massachusetts and New England, where we are based. Whether a property qualifies depends on its state, property type and the program.

What property types do you finance?

Single-family homes and 2 to 4 unit properties are our core, including condos and townhomes held as investments. Larger multifamily, mixed-use and condo development projects are reviewed case by case.

Who actually funds my loan?

We place each loan with one of our institutional capital partners, chosen for your deal. 316 Capital stays your single point of contact from pricing through closing and draws.

Does getting terms cost anything or pull my credit?

No. Send the deal and we tell you where it prices at no cost and with no credit pull. A full application and underwriting come later, once you decide to move forward.

How fast will I hear back?

Terms usually come back within one business day once we have the address, purchase price, budget and target loan amount. Missing details slow the answer, so send what you have up front.

What do I need to send to get priced?

The property address, purchase price or current value, renovation or construction budget, target loan amount, your exit plan and a short note on your experience. The fix and flip requirements guide walks through each item.

What interest rate will I get?

It depends on the program, your experience, credit, leverage and the property, and that applies to construction loans in Massachusetts too. Current ranges are on our rates page, and your term sheet gives the exact rate for your deal.

Does my credit score change my pricing?

It can. A stronger score can improve pricing or leverage, but credit is weighed together with your experience, cash and the property. Tell us about any credit issues early so we can point you to the right fit.

Can I get a loan on my first investment project?

Often, yes, but first projects are reviewed case by case. A clear scope, a qualified contractor, enough cash and a realistic exit matter most. See the first-time investor guide.

Is a hard money loan safe for a first-time investor?

It can be, if you go in with your eyes open. Read the written terms, know your full cash needs, keep a reserve for delays and have a realistic sale or refinance plan before you buy. The guide to choosing a lender covers what to verify.

What counts as experience?

Completed projects you owned or ran: flips, new builds and rentals you renovated and held. List each address, purchase and sale or refinance date, and what you did. Your point of contact will confirm how far back to go for your program.

Do I need an LLC?

Most investment loans close in an entity such as an LLC, but the requirement depends on the program. Confirm what is needed before you form or change an entity, and talk to your legal or tax adviser about the setup.

Do you need my tax returns or pay stubs?

Often not in the way a bank does. Many investor loans focus on the property, your cash and your experience, and a DSCR rental loan qualifies mainly on the property's rent. Your file checklist will list exactly what is required.

How much will you lend against the purchase, rehab and ARV?

It depends on the program, your experience and the property. Lenders cap the loan against total project cost (LTC) and the after-repair value (ARV), and the lower result usually controls. See ARV, LTC and LTV explained.

What is ARV?

ARV, or after-repair value, is the appraiser's estimate of what the property will be worth once the planned work is done. It is a value estimate, not a loan amount or a guaranteed sale price.

How much cash do I need to close?

Ask for an itemized cash-to-close estimate. Plan for your down payment, closing costs, reserves, carrying costs and work you pay for before each draw is reimbursed. Renovation money held back for draws is not cash for the purchase.

What does a hard money loan cost?

Interest, points and fees over the time you hold the loan. Compare quotes on the same loan amount and hold period, not just the rate. The cost guide shows the math.

What are points?

A point is 1% of the loan amount, usually paid at closing. Two points on a $500,000 loan is $10,000. Origination points are the main upfront financing charge, so compare them along with the rate.

What fees will I pay at closing?

Expect origination points plus third-party costs such as the appraisal, title, the closing attorney and insurance. Some programs also charge underwriting or processing fees. Your term sheet lists every fee, and you can ask for an itemized closing estimate.

What will my monthly payment be?

Most short-term investor loans are interest-only. The payment is the loan balance times the rate, divided by 12. For illustration only, $400,000 at 10% interest-only is about $3,333 a month. DSCR rental loans usually amortize, so their payment includes principal.

Is there a prepayment penalty?

It depends on the loan. Some short-term loans have minimum interest or exit fees, and some DSCR loans have a prepayment schedule. Ask for a payoff estimate at your expected exit date before you sign.

Does the loan cover the renovation?

Renovation and construction loans usually include a holdback for the work, released in draws as it is completed. Confirm which costs are covered and how much you fund yourself.

How do construction draws work?

You complete a phase of work, request a draw, an inspector confirms the work, and funds are released. Plan to pay for each phase before it is reimbursed. See how construction draws work.

Do I pay interest on rehab money I have not drawn yet?

It depends on the loan. Some charge interest only on funds drawn, others on the full commitment. Check the interest terms in your proposal.

What happens after I accept the term sheet?

You complete the application, pay for the appraisal, upload your documents and set up insurance while title and the closing attorney do their work. Underwriting reviews the file, then issues clear to close and closing is scheduled.

Who pays for the appraisal, and when?

You do, usually up front once you accept terms, because the appraisal is ordered before underwriting finishes. The cost depends on the property type, size and location, and your point of contact sends the payment request.

What if the appraisal comes in low?

The loan is usually resized to the accepted value, which can mean bringing more cash or adjusting the deal. You can ask about a value review if you have strong comparable sales. See ARV, LTC and LTV explained.

What insurance do I need?

Usually property coverage and liability, or builder's risk for construction, with the lender named on the policy. Get the exact written requirements from your point of contact and send the quote for review before you bind it.

Can you finance a property with a tenant in place?

Often, yes, especially for rental and bridge loans. For a renovation, tell us the occupancy and lease status up front, because tenants can change the program, the scope and the timeline.

What documents will I need?

Expect entity documents, ID, bank statements, the purchase contract, your budget and scope of work, and insurance. The exact list depends on your file. Start with the investor loan document checklist.

How fast can you close?

It depends on the file: appraisal, title, insurance and your documents. Tell us your contract deadline on day one and we will tell you honestly whether it is realistic. See how fast a loan can close.

What does clear to close mean?

All underwriting conditions have been accepted and the final loan documents are approved, so the closing can be scheduled. Until then, a closing date is a target.

Do I need an attorney to close in Massachusetts?

Yes. Massachusetts mortgage closings involve a closing attorney. Bring in your own real estate counsel early, especially on a tight contract deadline.

Can I refinance into a rental loan after the renovation?

Often, yes, with a DSCR rental loan. It is a separate underwriting decision, so plan it before you buy and check the rent, value and seasoning requirements. See how to finance a BRRRR deal.

What is a DSCR loan?

A rental property loan that qualifies mainly on whether the property's rent covers the loan payment, taxes, insurance and dues, rather than your personal income. See the DSCR loan requirements.

Do you offer DSCR cash-out refinances?

Yes. DSCR programs can include cash-out refinances on rental properties you own, subject to leverage, value, rent coverage and how long you have owned the property.

What if my project runs late or over budget?

Tell your point of contact as soon as you see it coming. Extra funds and extensions are not automatic, so keep a cash contingency and review your options early.
