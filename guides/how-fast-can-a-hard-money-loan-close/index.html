How fast can a hard money loan close in Massachusetts?

A hard money or bridge loan in Massachusetts typically closes in 10 to 14 business days, and 7 business days is achievable when title, insurance and valuation are lined up before the application. Three to five day closings happen but require an existing title policy, a broker price opinion instead of an appraisal, and a borrower with documents ready on day one. DSCR rental loans take longer, usually 3 to 5 weeks, because of the full appraisal and rent analysis.

The four things that decide the closing date

  1. Title. Massachusetts is an attorney closing state. The lender's attorney or your closing attorney orders a title examination, which takes 3 to 7 business days on a typical property and longer on estates, foreclosures, or properties with old undischarged mortgages. If the seller has a recent owner's policy, the exam can be updated in a day or two.
  2. Valuation. A broker price opinion comes back in 2 to 4 days. A full appraisal takes 7 to 14 days in Greater Boston and can run longer in the fall. Many loans under $1 million can close on a BPO.
  3. Insurance. Vacant property or builder's risk coverage with the lender named as mortgagee has to be bound before funding. Investors who call their agent on day one close a week earlier than those who call after the clear-to-close.
  4. Borrower documents. Entity documents, ID, two months of bank statements, the purchase agreement and the scope of work. Files where these arrive together on day one close in half the time of files where they trickle in.

What slows a Massachusetts closing

  • Estate sales where the license to sell or probate documents are not in hand
  • Multi-family properties with no certificate of occupancy history or open building violations
  • Condominiums that need a 6(d) certificate and trust documents from the association
  • Sellers who did not have their own attorney engaged when the contract was signed
  • Entities formed out of state without a Massachusetts foreign qualification, or with members who are not available to sign
  • Wire fraud verification, which is a good delay: every closing attorney confirms wire instructions by phone

Common questions

Can a hard money loan close in 3 days?

Occasionally. It requires a property with a recent title policy that can be updated quickly, a broker price opinion rather than an appraisal, insurance already bound, a borrower with entity and bank documents in hand, and a capital source willing to fund on that basis. Plan on 7 to 10 business days and treat 3 as a bonus.

Does Massachusetts require an attorney to close a hard money loan?

Yes. Massachusetts practice requires an attorney to conduct the closing and handle the title and recording. Budget a closing attorney fee and expect the attorney to run the title examination, which is usually the longest single step.

Do I need a full appraisal?

Not always. Many bridge and fix and flip loans under about $1 million close on a broker price opinion or a desktop valuation, which takes days rather than weeks. Construction loans and DSCR loans generally require a full appraisal.

What can I do today to close faster?

Form the LLC, gather two months of bank statements, get a written scope of work from the contractor, ask your insurance agent for a builder's risk or vacant property quote, and ask the seller whether they have an existing owner's title policy.

How long does a DSCR refinance take?

Three to five weeks is typical. The full appraisal with a rent schedule is the long pole. Ordering it the day terms are accepted and having the lease and tax and insurance bills ready keeps it near three.
