What does Cambridge's multifamily zoning change mean for real estate investors?

The short answer

Since February 2025, Cambridge has allowed four-story multifamily buildings by right in residential districts, and projects of nine units or fewer stay under the 20% affordable housing requirement. On September 28, 2026, the City Council voted to have staff draft tighter setback, open space and large-unit height rules, with a final vote expected by mid-June 2027. Projects with permits are best placed. Projects in design should plan for the stricter rules and build to condo standard so each unit can be sold or held.

What Cambridge changed in 2025

The Multifamily Housing Zoning Ordinance passed the City Council 8 to 1 on February 10, 2025. For a typical residential lot it changed three things.

  • Four stories by right. Multifamily buildings up to four stories are allowed in residential districts without a special permit.
  • Six stories with affordable units. On lots of at least 5,000 square feet, a building can reach six stories if 20% of the units are affordable.
  • Old limits removed. Minimum lot size per unit, unit-count caps and floor-area limits no longer apply to these projects.

Why nine units is the sweet spot

Cambridge's inclusionary housing rule requires 20% affordable units on projects of 10 or more units or 10,000 or more square feet. Stay at nine units and under that size, and every unit is market rate.

That line shaped the market. Since the law passed, a wave of permits has gone to four-story buildings with three to nine units, many with large units of well over 1,500 square feet. A lot that once held a two-family can now hold several large condos, and land values moved with it.

What the council started changing in September 2026

The new buildings drew pushback over size, shade and lost yard space, and a petition for a moratorium gathered close to 5,000 signatures. On September 28, 2026, the council voted unanimously to have city staff draft amendments.

RuleUnder discussion
Side setbacksAt least 15 feet combined
Front and rear setbacksAt least 25 feet combined
Open spaceRaised from 15% to 20% of the lot
Height for large unitsPossibly three stories when units average roughly 1,600 to 2,000 square feet or more

None of this is law yet. Staff proposals are due December 7, 2026, and the council expects a final vote no later than mid-June 2027. Projects that already hold permits are in the best position. Projects still in design carry rule risk until the vote.

Why Cambridge is its own market

Cambridge has held its value through a soft 2026 better than its neighbors. Demand comes from institutions that do not leave, in a city that has built little housing for decades.

  • Prices held. Through July 2026 the median Cambridge condo price slipped 1.8% to $993,000 while Somerville's fell 6.5%, and the median single-family price rose 1.9% to about $2.48 million, according to Cambridge Day using Massachusetts Association of Realtors data.
  • Rentals stay full. Cambridge's real-time rental vacancy rate is about 1%, according to the Boston Pads 2026 Cambridge rental report.
  • Demand is anchored. Harvard, MIT, the hospitals and life science employers keep a steady flow of students, faculty and staff looking for housing.
  • Supply stays tight. If the council tightens the 2025 law, fewer new projects get built and permitted projects become more valuable.

The risks are real too. In March 2026 the median Cambridge condo spent 88 days on market, up from 42 a year earlier, according to the Massachusetts Association of Realtors. Office vacancy reached 26.9% in the second quarter of 2026, according to CBRE, which can soften demand from tech and lab workers. A good plan still works if a sale takes six months.

The nine-unit playbook for a slow condo market

Nine units gives you more than one way out. Build to condo standard, then decide unit by unit whether to sell or hold. Because every unit is market rate, every unit can be sold or rented at full value.

ExitHow it worksWhen it fits
Sell some, keep someSell enough units to pay off the construction loan and rent the restA slow condo market, when you want cash back and long-term upside
Sell allComplete the condominium documents and sell each unitA strong selling season and a quick payoff
Hold and rent allRefinance into a long-term rental loanRents strong, sale prices soft
Rent now, sell laterLease for a year or two, then sell into a better marketPatient capital and a longer time horizon

In today's market, selling some and keeping some is the strongest default. Sales repay the most expensive money in the deal, and the units you keep earn rent and can be sold later at better prices. A rental refinance is its own underwriting, so test rents, expenses and debt coverage before you count on it.

  1. Plan as condos from day one. Separate utilities, condo-grade finishes and condominium documents ready at completion keep the sale option open. The condo financing guide covers unit releases and sales cash flow.
  2. Mix unit sizes. A few smaller units sell and rent faster than all large ones, and they fit the large-unit rules under discussion.
  3. Underwrite at today's prices. Use current medians, not 2022 peaks, and assume a slow sale.
  4. Know your takeout before you finish. Have rental refinance terms in hand so a slow sale never forces a discount. See the DSCR loan requirements.
  5. Check the rules before you lease. Ask a Massachusetts attorney about local rules on converting rented units to condos before you rent a unit you may later sell.

How we financed one in Cambridge

In November 2026, 316 Capital closed financing for a repeat client buying a two-family in Cambridge. The financing was built in two stages.

Stage one: get control fast. The purchase was financed with a fix and flip loan, so the client closed on the seller's timeline instead of waiting on a full construction approval.

Stage two: refinance into construction. Once the plans are final, the client refinances into a ground-up construction loan sized to the finished building, with funds released in draws as work is completed.

The short loan won the property. The larger loan arrives when the plan is final, so the client is not paying for construction money before it is needed. And when the building is done, the client can sell, hold or both. Because this is new construction, the rule changes above matter here too, which is why permit timing is part of the plan.

What investors should do now

  • If you hold a permit, build. Projects permitted under the current rules have the clearest path.
  • If you are in design, plan for the stricter rules. Draw a version with wider setbacks and 20% open space. If it still works, the rule change is a smaller risk.
  • Re-check the large-unit plan. If your numbers depend on four stories of 2,000 square foot units, model a three-story version too.
  • Use a two-step loan. Buy with a short bridge or fix and flip loan, then refinance into construction once plans are final.
  • Line up financing before the vote. Lenders price rule risk. A term sheet in hand before June 2027 removes one unknown.

Common questions

Does Cambridge allow multifamily buildings by right?

Yes. Since February 10, 2025, multifamily buildings up to four stories are allowed by right in Cambridge residential districts. On lots of at least 5,000 square feet, six stories are allowed if 20% of the units are affordable.

Why do so many new Cambridge projects have nine units or fewer?

Cambridge requires 20% affordable units on projects of 10 or more units or 10,000 or more square feet. At nine units and under that size, every unit is market rate.

What did the Cambridge City Council vote on in September 2026?

On September 28, 2026, the council voted unanimously to have staff draft amendments covering wider setbacks, open space raised from 15% to 20% and a possible three-story limit for buildings with very large units. None of it is law yet.

When will the new Cambridge zoning rules be decided?

Staff proposals are due December 7, 2026, and the council expects a final vote no later than mid-June 2027.

What is the best exit for a small Cambridge condo project in a slow market?

Building to condo standard and selling some units while renting the rest keeps the most options open. Sales repay the construction loan, and the kept units can be refinanced into a rental loan if they qualify and sold later.
